**Bottom line [EST]**
- **Automation recovery.** All three left the 2023–25 automation destocking trough in 2026. Siemens DI's margin rebounded from 14.9% to about 18–19%, and Schneider IA returned to high-single-digit organic growth.
- **Growth is coming from electrification, not automation.** The data-centre and AI-driven electrification boom (Schneider EM +18.8% organic, ABB Electrification, Siemens Smart Infrastructure) now drives group growth and valuations. Factory automation is the slower, more cyclical, more China-exposed part of each group.
- **Siemens.** The purest large-scale bet on factory automation plus industrial software (DI ≈ €18bn, ARR €5.7bn).
- **Schneider.** Turning itself into an industrial-software company: AVEVA, then PTC at ~$24bn. IA hardware is a small share of the group (about 16%).
- **ABB.** Exiting robots and focusing on process automation and field devices (Rotork). Discrete/machine automation sits in B&R inside the Automation business area.

### 1b. Strategy and portfolio

**M&A and divestitures**
- **Siemens Energy.** Spun off in 2020 [R\*].
- **Healthineers.** Stake sell-downs, with 4% sold in Q4 FY25 for €1.9bn [R]. A direct spin-off of about 30% to shareholders is planned, leaving a roughly 37% minority stake [R]. Siemens has binding tax rulings, and the AGM votes are expected in **February 2027** [R].
- **Innomotics.** The large-drives and motors business was sold to KPS for about €3.5bn in 2024 [R\*].
- **Software buys.**
  - Mendix (2018, ~$0.7bn), Supplyframe (2021), Brightly (2022, ~$1.6bn) [R\*].
  - **Altair** (closed March 2025, ~$10bn EV; simulation/HPC/AI) [R\*].
  - **Dotmatics** (closed 2025, ~$5.1bn; R&D software for life sciences; €4.2bn cash out in Q4 FY25) [R].
  - Heliox (EV charging, 2024) [R].
- **Rationale.** Build a "ONE Tech Company" that combines automation, software and AI. Healthineers is deconsolidated to free up capital-allocation flexibility [R].

**Strategic programme**
- "ONE Tech Company," presented at Capital Markets Day in Dec 2025 [R]:
  - Revenue growth target raised to "up to 9%" p.a., excluding Healthineers (was up to 7%).
  - Adjusted EPS to grow at a high-single-digit rate; progressive dividend.
  - Four pillars: Grow Digital, Grow Regions, Grow Verticals, Grow AI.

**China**
- China is DI's second-largest automation market. The local-for-local "China portfolio" grew about 30% YTD in FY26 [R].
- **Competition from domestic vendors is the main structural threat [R] [EST]:**
  - Domestic brands (Inovance and others) held about 55% of China's core-automation market in 2025, up from about 30% in 2020.
  - Inovance alone has about 32% of China servo (No. 1).
  - In small PLCs, the domestic share is about 41% (2024).
  - In medium and large PLCs, the domestic share is only about 8%. This is still Siemens' fortress segment.

- **Locations.** Corporate strategy and M&A are in **Munich**. Software, DISW and Altair are run substantially from the US (Plano TX, Troy MI) [R\*].

### 1d. Technology
- **Platform and portfolio.**
  - Siemens Xcelerator: an open digital-business platform and marketplace.
  - TIA Portal; SIMATIC S7-1500.
  - **SIMATIC S7-1500V virtual PLC.** A software-defined PLC running on industrial edge or servers, launched 2024–25 [R\*].
- **AI.**
  - **Industrial Copilot**, built with Microsoft, for generating PLC code [R\*].
  - FY26: launched "Intelligence Center X" for orchestrating AI agents. The "Eigen" engineering agent has hundreds of customers in 30+ countries [R].
- **Software.**
  - The strongest PLM/EDA/simulation stack among the peers: NX, Teamcenter, Opcenter, Mentor/EDA, and Altair simulation [R\*].
- **Verdict [EST].** Siemens is the clear global leader in discrete PLCs and engineering software combined. It is the reference platform that peers measure against.

### 1e. Downturn resilience [EST]
- The DI margin floor in the last trough was 14.9% for FY25. The quarterly low was around 13–14%, with M&A and severance noise.
- That floor is far above loss-making territory.
- The group has diversified earnings (Smart Infrastructure, Mobility, the Healthineers stake), record FCF of €10.8bn and an A+ rating. Resilience is high.
- The risk is in people rather than solvency. Siemens cuts DI capacity promptly when volumes drop, as with the 5,600 cuts in 2025.

  - €250m cost synergies by Year 3 and about €800m revenue synergies.

- **China.** China & East Asia grew 26% organic in Q3 '26 on data centres. IA China grew high-single-digit; process industries fell. Schneider faces the same domestic-substitution pressure in low-end PLC, VFD and servo as Siemens [EST].

### 2c. People
- **Headcount.** About 150–160k [R\*]. The company describes "150,000 colleagues" [R]. No IA-only headcount is disclosed.
- **Leadership.**
  - **CEO Olivier Blum** since Nov 2024. He replaced Peter Herweck, who was removed after about 18 months over strategy-execution disagreements [R\*/R].
  - Jean-Pascal Tricoire is Chairman [R].
- **Locations.** Global HQ is Rueil-Malmaison (Paris). Schneider runs a multi-hub model with major leadership presence in Hong Kong, Boston/Andover (North America) and Bangalore. AVEVA's HQ is Cambridge, UK [R\*].
- **Cuts.** These are targeted plant consolidations rather than headline mass layoffs. AVEVA had post-acquisition integration cuts in 2023–24 [R\*].

### 2d. Technology
- **EcoStruxure** is Schneider's IoT/edge/apps architecture.
- **EcoStruxure Automation Expert** is the first commercial **IEC 61499 software-defined, hardware-agnostic** automation system. Schneider founded and backs **UniversalAutomation.org** [R\*].
- **AVEVA** covers PI System, MES, simulation and AVEVA Connect (cloud). It is strong in process industries [R\*].
- PTC would add Creo, Windchill, ThingWorx/Kepware and Servigistics [EST].
- **Verdict [EST].**
  - Schneider is the ideological leader in open, software-defined control.
  - It trails Siemens badly in installed PLC share in discrete manufacturing.
  - It is strong in process and hybrid software through AVEVA.

### 2e. Downturn resilience [EST]
- IA's trough margin of 14.2% is respectable, and the group margin kept rising through the automation downturn thanks to Energy Management.
- Net debt is rising to about 2× EBITDA with PTC, but the rating is A/A2.
- Group resilience is very high. IA-specific resilience is moderate: it is a small, lower-margin segment, and the PTC integration may trigger overlap cuts in 2027–28.

- **China.** About 15% of group revenue [R\*]. China reported orders were −11% in Q4 2024 [R]. Chinese robot makers (Estun, Inovance, Efort) took share in robots, which was a key reason to exit [EST].

### 3c. People
- **Headcount.** About 110,000 employees [R]. Robotics transfers about 7,000 to SoftBank on closing [R\*].

- **Locations.** HQ is Zurich (Oerlikon). B&R is at Eggelsberg (Austria). Process Automation has major hubs in Mannheim, Västerås, Houston and Singapore. Robotics was run from Shanghai and Västerås [R\*].
- **Cuts.** ABB runs a decentralised "ABB Way" model. Restructuring happens by division (Machine Automation underabsorption in 2024; E-mobility) [R]. No group-level mass layoff was announced in 2024–26 [R\*/EST].

### 3d. Technology
- **Process control.** ABB Ability System 800xA. ABB is No. 1 or 2 globally in DCS alongside Emerson and Honeywell [R\*].
- **"Automation Extended."** An evolution path for DCS toward software-defined control (2024) [R\*].
- **Machine control.** B&R Automation Studio; ACOPOStrak/SuperTrak intelligent track systems [R\*].
- **AI.** ABB Ability Genix Industrial Copilot [R\*].
- **Robotics.** OmniCore controllers and AMRs (ex-ASTI). These are **leaving with Robotics to SoftBank** [R\*].
- **Verdict [EST].** ABB leads in process automation and electrification. It is a niche player in discrete PLC/machine control via B&R, and is not a platform leader in factory automation.

### 3e. Downturn resilience [EST]
- Highest-quality balance sheet of the three: net debt/EBITDA 0.3; about $4.8bn of robotics proceeds coming in; record margins and ROCE.
- The 2023–24 automation trough hit only RA (margin floor about 8%). The group still printed record margins thanks to Electrification and Process.
- **Very high resilience at group level.** Discrete-automation jobs at B&R are the most cyclical.

### 4.2 Who leads in what [EST]
- **Discrete PLC/automation and engineering software (PLM, simulation): Siemens.** It is No. 1 by a wide margin and the most "pure" factory-automation employer at scale.
- **Open/software-defined control (IEC 61499) and process software (AVEVA), plus CAD/PLM after PTC: Schneider.** This is the fastest-moving software bet.
- **DCS/process automation and field devices (plus Rotork): ABB.** ABB is exiting robots.
- **Growth engine for all three: electrification for data centres and AI.**
  - Schneider EM +18.8%, ABB Electrification orders >$7bn per quarter, Siemens Smart Infrastructure +10–11%.
  - Electrification now outgrows automation at all three.

### 4.3 Career and strategy-role implications [EST]
- **Corporate strategy and M&A** sits at:
  - Siemens: Munich, with a strong US software M&A presence.
  - Schneider: Rueil-Malmaison/Paris, plus Boston/Andover and the PTC base in Boston from 2027–28.
  - ABB: Zurich, with decentralised division strategy teams.
- **Deal activity is high at all three in 2025–27,** so demand for corp-dev and integration talent is elevated:
  - Siemens: Altair/Dotmatics integration, Healthineers spin.
  - Schneider: PTC, Shelly, SEIPL.
  - ABB: Robotics carve-out, Rotork, Mitsubishi JV.
- **Layoff risk is highest** in Siemens DI hardware and Germany (cut 8% in 2025), ABB B&R/Machine Automation, and post-PTC overlap areas at Schneider in 2027–28.
- **Layoff risk is lowest** in electrification and data-centre business units at all three.
