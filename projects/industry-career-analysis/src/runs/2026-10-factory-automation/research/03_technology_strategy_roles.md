## 0. Executive summary

1. **The industry is turning from hardware into software and AI, and that is a strategy problem.** Leaders are buying software at large scale and selling slower hardware or non-core assets. In the 24 months to Oct 2026, the verified moves are:
   - Siemens bought Altair and Dotmatics (about €14bn) [R].
   - Emerson bought out AspenTech ($7.2bn for the minority) [R].
   - ABB sold Robotics to SoftBank ($5.4bn) [R].
   - Honeywell split into three companies (completed 29 Jun 2026) [R].
   - Schneider bought out its India JV (€5.5bn) [R] and agreed to buy **PTC for an EV of $23.7bn** (5 Oct 2026, with a €6bn equity raise on 20 Oct 2026) [R].
   This is one of the most active portfolio-reshaping periods the sector has had. Corporate strategy, M&A and integration skills are core to it, not a support function [D].
2. **Next-wave technology:** industrial AI and copilots, AI-native digital twins (NVIDIA Omniverse), virtual or software-defined PLCs, physical AI in robots, and OT cybersecurity. Together they push the business model toward ARR, platforms and ecosystems.
   - Siemens Digital Industries ARR reached **€5.3bn** at Q4 FY25 (+14%) [R].
   - Schneider says software and services will be about **24% of group revenue** pro forma with PTC [R].
   - Rockwell is guiding to high-single-digit recurring-revenue growth [R].
3. **The most strategy-intensive employers** (2024–26 evidence) are Schneider, Siemens, Honeywell, Emerson and ABB, followed by Hitachi and Rockwell. **The most operations-led** are Keyence, Fanuc and Yaskawa, plus Omron in a restructuring sense [D].
4. **Hiring signal:** strategy and M&A roles are being posted.
   - Siemens DI Software: "Principal, M&A Strategist (Inorganic Strategy)", US remote, Jun 2026, base $147.5–206.6k plus a 10–15% bonus [R].
   - ABB Corporate Strategy & M&A, Zurich: internship posting Feb 2026, which confirms the team runs strategy, portfolio management, acquisitions and divestments [R].
5. **Rising skills:** AI and software business models (ARR/SaaS economics), ecosystem and alliance strategy (NVIDIA, Microsoft, AWS), large-deal integration (PTC, Altair, AspenTech), separation and spin execution, China competitive strategy (Chinese robot makers now hold 57% of their home market [R]), and India localisation.

## PART A — Technology roadmap 2026–2031

### A1. What drove growth historically [M/D]

| Era | Core technology | Value-capture model | Leaders |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1970s–90s | PLC, DCS, CNC | Proprietary hardware plus locked-in programming environments | Siemens (S5/S7), Allen-Bradley/Rockwell (Logix), Mitsubishi, Fanuc (CNC), Honeywell/Emerson/ABB/Yokogawa (DCS) |
| 1990s–2010s | Servo/motion, VFD drives, industrial Ethernet, robots | Hardware volume, installed-base lock-in, distributor channels | Yaskawa, Fanuc, ABB, KUKA, Siemens, Rockwell, Schneider |
| 2000s–2010s | Machine vision and sensors | High-margin direct sales of sensors and vision | Keyence, Cognex, Omron, Sick |
| 2010s–2020s | IIoT, MES/SCADA, PLM, simulation | Software licences moving to subscription | Siemens (UGS/Mentor/Altair), Dassault, PTC, AVEVA (Schneider), AspenTech (Emerson), Plex (Rockwell) |

**Industrial robots:** 542k units were installed in 2024, about twice the level of 10 years earlier. The operational stock is 4.66m. China took 54% of installs (295k), and **Chinese suppliers hold 57% of China's market**, up from about 28% ten years ago. IFR forecasts 575k units in 2025 and more than 700k by 2028 [R, IFR World Robotics 2025]. Japan (−4%), the US (−9%) and Germany (−5%) all fell in 2024 [R]. Robot growth is now Asia-led and price-competitive, which helps explain ABB's exit [D].

### A4. Which technologies change the business model, and the strategic agenda [D]

1. **Hardware to subscription (ARR).** The SaaS transition, as at Siemens, needs pricing and packaging strategy, management of the revenue dip, and new investor KPIs (ARR, NRR, cloud share).
2. **Platform ecosystems.** Xcelerator, EcoStruxure, Forge and the NVIDIA Omniverse alliances make partner strategy and alliance management a C-level topic. Value capture depends on who owns the data layer and the AI layer.
3. **Software-defined control (vPLC) separating hardware from software.** This threatens the most profitable franchise (Logix, S7). Incumbents must choose between cannibalising themselves and holding back, which is a classic portfolio decision.
4. **Physical AI** pulls in deep-pocketed non-traditional players (SoftBank, NVIDIA, Chinese OEMs). It forces build, partner, buy or exit decisions, and ABB chose to exit.
5. **Design to operations convergence (PLM + MES + simulation).** This is the logic behind Siemens–Altair, Schneider–PTC and Emerson–AspenTech. Expect more software consolidation, and with it more integration work for strategy and PMO teams.

### B2. Activism and the strategy agenda [R/D]

- **Elliott at Honeywell** (more than $5bn, Nov 2024) led to a full break-up completed in Jun 2026. This is the template case of activism forcing portfolio strategy.
- **Elliott at AspenTech** (9%, Feb 2025) tried to squeeze more value out of Emerson's buy-in and failed on price.
- Japanese companies (Mitsubishi Electric, Hitachi, Omron) face governance and TSE capital-efficiency pressure [M]. That is why Hitachi created a CFO-led Corporate Strategy Group and Mitsubishi Electric is pushing "value enhancement" [R].

### B5. Rising skills for strategy professionals, 2026–2031 [D, grounded in the evidence above]

1. **Software and AI business models:** ARR/NRR economics, SaaS transitions, AI monetisation and pricing (Siemens ARR, Schneider at 24% software).
2. **Large-deal M&A and PMI:** synergy capture (Schneider–PTC targets €250m cost and about €800m revenue synergies [R]), purchase-price allocation and integration governance.
3. **Separation and spin execution:** Honeywell, ABB Robotics, the Siemens Healthineers spin, Schneider divestments.
4. **Ecosystem and alliance strategy:** NVIDIA, Microsoft, AWS, Meta (Siemens CES 2026 partners [R]).
5. **China and Asia competitive strategy:** Chinese robot makers at 57% of their home market, China at 54% of global installs [R]. India localisation (Schneider's India hub) [R].
6. **Physical AI and robotics venture strategy:** build, partner or exit choices, and corporate venture capital.
7. **Activist and investor-facing strategy:** CMD target design and capital allocation narratives.

### B6. Exit options for a senior strategy professional (brief) [D/M]

- **Private equity, industrial-tech focus.** Examples: Blackstone (Copeland), One Rock, KKR, Carlyle, Apollo; also mid-market funds that buy carve-outs like the €1–1.5bn Schneider divestments. Roles: operating partner, portfolio-strategy VP, carve-out lead.
- **Consulting.** Strategy firms' industrials, Industry 4.0 and M&A practices; also Big-4 deal advisory.
- **Corporate development in adjacent sectors:** industrial software (Dassault, Autodesk, Bentley, Hexagon), semiconductors (NVIDIA, Qualcomm industrial edge), energy and electrification (Eaton, Vertiv, Hitachi Energy), data centres.
- **Robotics and physical-AI startups and acquirers:** SoftBank Robotics/ABB Robotics post-close, humanoid start-ups. Roles: chief of staff, head of strategy, BD.
- **Transferability is high,** because portfolio, M&A and software-transition skills are sector-agnostic. A 15-year profile plus factory-automation domain knowledge is especially valued in PE and software corp dev [D].
