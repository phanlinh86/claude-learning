# 08 — Employee Feedback, Layoffs & Long-Term Working Conditions (Factory Automation)

**Lens:** a senior corporate-strategy / strategic-planning professional with 15 years of experience. The question is how stable a senior HQ strategy role is over 10–20 years.
**As of:** 28 Oct 2026. **Searches used:** ~22 of the shared budget.
**Conventions:**
- **[V]** means verified in a source fetched for this report.
- **[PK]** means prior knowledge that wasn't re-verified this run. Treat it as likely but check it.
- **[EST]** means an analyst estimate or inference.

Reviews are paraphrased throughout.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **The 2023–25 destocking cycle caused real cuts, and they fell hardest on SG&A and HQ functions.** The clearest cases:
  - Rockwell cut about 3% of staff (~870–900) in May 2024, mainly sales, marketing and HQ. Headcount fell from ~29k (fall 2023) to 26k (Sept 2025), about −10% [V].
  - Siemens Digital Industries is cutting ~5,600 jobs, 2,600 of them in Germany, by Sept 2027. German cuts are being made without compulsory redundancies [V].
  - Omron cut ~2,000 jobs in 2024, split 1,000 Japan and 1,000 overseas. It called this the second major layoff in its 90-year history [V].
- **Japanese "profit-making restructuring" has arrived.** Mitsubishi Electric posted record profit in FY3/2025, then ran a voluntary early-retirement program for staff aged 53+. It drew 2,378 applicants, with about 4,700 expected group-wide [V]. Senior, older, non-line staff are exactly the target.
- **Portfolio change is now the biggest structural risk for HQ strategy roles, more than cyclical demand.**
  - Honeywell is splitting into three companies. Solstice has already been spun; the Aerospace spin was pulled forward to 2H 2026 [V/PK].
  - ABB is selling Robotics (~7,000 employees) to SoftBank for $5.375bn, closing mid-to-late 2026. It is also reorganizing into three business areas [V].
  - Emerson is consolidating its NI and Intelligent Platforms acquisitions, including closing the Charlottesville site by end-2026 [V].
  - Spins and sales often create new strategy jobs at first, then consolidate corporate centers 12–24 months later [EST].
- **Employee sentiment (Glassdoor, mid-2026 snapshot) [V]:**
  - Schneider leads at 4.2, with 87% recommend and 90% CEO approval.
  - Siemens 4.1 / 81% / 90%.
  - Honeywell 4.1 / 84% / 88%.
  - ABB 4.0 / 80% / 86%.
  - Emerson 3.7 / 75% / 79%.
  - Rockwell is weakest at 3.7, with 72% recommend, 70% CEO approval and only 59% positive business outlook.
- **Best long-term stability for senior strategy [EST]:** Schneider and Siemens (European social model, scale, electrification and data-center tailwind), followed by Keyence and Fanuc for stability, though they are poor fits for a non-Japanese-speaking HQ strategist.
- **Highest-churn picks [EST]:** Honeywell (multi-year separation), Emerson (serial M&A integration, tenure-based RIFs reported) and Rockwell (US at-will plus repeated SG&A cuts).

### 3.2 Recurring themes

**Schneider Electric**
- Pros: pay and benefits, hybrid work and PTO, travel, culture and WLB, a strong sustainability/electrification narrative.
- Cons: progression favors credentials, limited influence over processes, pay "could be better." The heavily matrixed global structure and Herweck's 2024 ouster point to strategy-level turbulence at the top [PK].

**Siemens**
- Pros: CEO seen as morale-positive, good benefits, job security in Germany.
- Cons (recurring on Glassdoor/Kununu historically [PK]): bureaucracy, slow decisions, middle-management politics, opaque processes. One 2026 review cites "toxic" middle managers and unfair workload.
- For strategy roles, the sheer size gives deep internal mobility (DI, SI, Mobility, Financial Services, Siemens Advanta consulting) but slow promotion [PK].

**ABB**
- Pros: culture, pace, fair pay.
- Cons: chaotic at times; many stakeholders across HQ and countries.
- The decentralized "ABB Way" means a thin group HQ, so fewer central strategy seats; the strategy work sits in business areas [PK].

**Honeywell**
- Polarized.
- Positive reviews: strong brand, a customer and performance focus, collaborative.
- Negative reviews (2026): quarter-to-quarter short-termism, cut-throat culture, open floor plans, senior leaders demeaning staff, high turnover.
- Complex organization and heavy internal process. The "Honeywell Operating System" and metric intensity are a long-standing theme [PK].

**Emerson**
- Pros: supportive managers, WLB, growth.
- Cons: slow and powerful HR; tenure-based RIF selection; a complacent culture; executive pay optics during cuts.

**Rockwell**
- Pros: good culture and people, Milwaukee-centric collegiality.
- Cons: limited on-site opportunities; layoff anxiety in acquired businesses (AMR).
- The lowest outlook score (59%) reflects 2024–25 order weakness and repeated SG&A cuts.

**Keyence (OpenWork)**
- One Oct-2026 review from a business-role employee with 3–5 years' tenure scores 4.0. It reports pay of ~¥15M, ~60 hours/month overtime and only 15% paid-leave use.
- That reviewer rates growth in one's twenties, fairness of evaluation and compliance very high, but internal openness very low (2.0). Frequent transfers are a lifestyle drawback.
- Historical average pay was ~¥18.4M (2020 securities report) versus Mitsubishi Electric ~¥8.1M and Omron ~¥8.3M at the time. Bonuses are paid quarterly and tied to profit.
- The culture is rational and results-driven, with intense pressure [V].

**Japanese firms in general (Fanuc, Yaskawa, Omron, Mitsubishi Electric)** [PK]
- Seniority-based promotion and Japanese-language decision-making.
- Corporate-planning (経営企画) roles go to long-tenured insiders, so mid-career foreign hires seldom reach HQ strategy leadership.
- Fanuc is notably closed: a remote Yamanashi HQ, a secretive culture and minimal IR.
- Mitsubishi Electric's 53+ program shows older staff are being actively thinned.

## 5. Sentiment shift 2025–2026 [EST unless noted]

- **Destocking recovery.** Orders bottomed in 2024–early 2025 and recovered through 2025–26, especially process, data-center and electrification. Rockwell's FY25 segment margin rose to 20.4% from 19.3% [V], mostly through productivity (i.e., fewer people) rather than rehiring. Expect "flat headcount, rising margin" to persist.
- **Electrification and data centers.** Schneider (and Siemens SI, ABB Electrification) carry the most positive narrative. That likely explains Schneider's top Glassdoor outlook (77%).
- **AI hype.** Siemens (Altair, Industrial Copilot), Rockwell and Emerson (AspenTech) are all hiring for software/AI. Strategy roles are migrating toward software M&A and partnership work, which is good for a strategist who can speak software.
- **Portfolio churn.** Honeywell's split, ABB's Robotics exit and Emerson's integration cycle all create a 2025–27 window of high strategy workload (separations, capital-markets days). They are followed by corporate-center slimming.
- **Japan.** Firms are shifting from lifetime employment to "profit-making restructuring" (Mitsubishi Electric, Omron, plus Panasonic's 12k as a peer signal [V]). Older white-collar staff are at risk even in good years.

## 6. Best / worst for long-term stability of a senior strategy role [EST]

Ranking (1 = most stable over 10–20 years for a senior corporate-strategy professional, assuming no Japanese fluency):

1. **Schneider Electric.**
   - Highest employee sentiment.
   - The structural tailwind (electrification, data centers) supports headcount.
   - French and EU labor protections mean big cuts tend to hit factories (PSE), not HQ strategy.
   - Risk: CEO-level volatility (2024).
2. **Siemens.**
   - German co-determination: no forced redundancies at German sites, and cuts go via attrition.
   - Huge internal mobility; a big, durable strategy function (corporate plus Siemens Advanta).
   - Risks: bureaucracy and slow promotion. A non-German-based role carries less protection (the US is at-will). Continued portfolio pruning (Healthineers).
3. **ABB.**
   - Strong balance sheet and electrification exposure.
   - But the thin-HQ philosophy leaves few senior strategy seats.
   - The Robotics sale removes a business area.
   - Good if positioned in Electrification BA strategy.
4. **Keyence / Fanuc.**
   - Near-zero layoff risk and fortress balance sheets.
   - But an HQ strategy role for a non-Japanese outsider is practically unavailable or a career dead-end. Keyence's intensity doesn't suit a 15-year veteran unless in a regional (overseas subsidiary) planning role.
5. **Rockwell Automation.**
   - A pure-play automation leader, but US at-will.
   - HQ and SG&A were explicitly targeted in 2024; ~10% headcount decline FY23→FY25.
   - Lowest CEO approval and outlook of the Western set.
   - Strategy work is focused and valuable, but each downturn means another SG&A cut.
6. **Emerson.**
   - Serial M&A (NI, AspenTech) produces integration-driven site closures and RIFs; one reviewer reports tenure-based selection.
   - Strategy and M&A roles are busy during deals, but the post-integration corporate center is lean.
7. **Omron / Mitsubishi Electric / Yaskawa.**
   - Omron has demonstrated willingness to cut 1,000 in Japan in a downturn.
   - Mitsubishi targets the 53+ cohort even at record profit.
   - Plus language and seniority barriers.
8. **Honeywell (worst for stability).**
   - A three-way split means duplicated, then rationalized, corporate functions and new standalone HQs.
   - A metrics-heavy, polarizing culture with recurring annual repositioning charges.
   - A strategist could gain a lot of separation experience, but the 10–20-year stability of any single seat is the weakest.
   - Inovance is a growth story but practically inaccessible (Chinese-language, Shenzhen-centric) and is excluded from the ranking.
