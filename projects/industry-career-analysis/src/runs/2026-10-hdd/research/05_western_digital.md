## 1. Executive summary

- **What WD is now.** On Feb 21, 2025 Western Digital spun off its Flash business as Sandisk [R]. WD is now a pure-play HDD company, rebranded "WD" in Feb 2026, still trading as WDC [R]. About 88-89% of revenue comes from Cloud (hyperscale nearline) [R].
- **FY2026 (ended Jul 3, 2026) was a record year** [R]:
  - Revenue $12.92B (+36%)
  - Non-GAAP gross margin 49.1%, up from 39.4% in FY2025
  - Non-GAAP operating income $4.82B (~37% margin)
  - Non-GAAP EPS $10.22
  - FCF $3.51B
  - Buybacks $2.59B
- **Q1 FY2027 (ended Oct 2, 2026) accelerated further** [R]:
  - Revenue $4.38B (+55% Y/Y)
  - Non-GAAP gross margin 58.1%; non-GAAP operating margin 49.1%
  - Non-GAAP EPS $4.64
  - FCF $1.38B
  - Shipments 248 EB (+22% Y/Y); price per TB +28% Y/Y [C]
- **Q2 FY2027 guidance** [R]:
  - Revenue $5.1B ±$0.1B (+66-72% Y/Y)
  - Gross margin 60-61%
  - EPS $5.80 ±$0.20
- **Balance sheet has been transformed** [R/C]:
  - Gross debt fell from ~$7.4B (FY24, incl. flash) and $4.7B (FY25) to $0.79B (Oct 2026).
  - WD is now net cash (~+$0.5B). The Sandisk stake has been fully monetized.
  - S&P and Fitch upgraded WD to investment grade (spring 2026).
- **Technology position** [C]:
  - WD leads in ePMR/CMR + UltraSMR capacity: 32TB shipping, and 40TB ePMR in volume from mid-2026.
  - It is the HAMR follower: 44TB HAMR qualification is due CQ1 2027, with volume in 1H CY2027.
  - Seagate has shipped HAMR (Mozaic) in volume since 2024-25 [M].
  - WD's roadmap targets 60TB ePMR, 100TB HAMR by 2029, and 140TB potential (14 platters × 10TB).
- **Investment view** [E]:
  - WD is a very high-quality, highly profitable duopoly asset at a cyclical peak.
  - Downturn resilience is far better than in FY2023: net cash, opex at only ~9% of revenue, multi-year LTAs, and no unit-capacity adds.
  - The key risks are HAMR execution vs. Seagate, hyperscaler concentration (top-3 = 44% of revenue), and cyclic pricing reversal.

**Full-year and mix data**

**Customer concentration** [R, 10-K]
- FY2026: three customers at 16%, 15% and 13%; top-10 = 73%.
- FY2025: 17%, 12% and 10%; top-10 = 68%.
- FY2024: no customer ≥10%.
- Concentration has risen sharply. That cuts both ways: LTAs bring visibility, but each customer gains bargaining power.

**Product mix** [C]
- ~90% of drives shipped are mass-capacity nearline.
- 4.1M ePMR drives (118 EB) shipped in Q3 FY26.
- The 40TB ePMR platform is expected to reach ~30% of nearline shipments in FQ2'27 and ~50% in FQ3'27.
- UltraSMR has been adopted by the 3 largest customers. WD targets ~60% of exabytes on UltraSMR by end of FY27.

**LTAs and visibility** [C]
- As of Feb 2026, LTAs with top customers ran through CY2027, one through CY2028.
- As of Apr 2026, LTAs extended into CY2028-2029. They are exabyte-based with set pricing; volume above commitments is priced separately.
- As of Aug 2026, talks were underway for agreements into 2029-2031.
- As of Oct 2026, the top 7 customers have visibility to 2030-31.
- Some LTAs include anniversary price escalators. A portion of volume is uncontracted and sold at higher spot prices.
- Manufacturing lead time is ~1 year, so purchase orders are placed about a year out.

**Supply discipline** [C]
- "No plans to add unit capacity." Exabyte growth comes from areal density, more platters (up to 14) and mix.
- Capex target is 4-6% of revenue on average over 5 years, modestly above that for the next 2 years. Spend goes to heads, media, automation and HAMR, not unit capacity.
- FY26 capex ≈ $418M (OCF − FCF, ~3.2% of revenue) [E]. Q1 FY27 capex was $227M (5.2%) [R].
- Management argued that competitors without in-house heads and media (implicitly Toshiba) may need to add units. An analyst asked about the "smallest player" adding capacity [C].

**Cost headwind** [C]: DRAM and NAND content in drives is rising in cost. Near-term cost/TB declines will fall short of the ~10%/yr long-term goal.

### 3.8 Downturn resilience

**FY2023 downturn (the stress test)**
- HDD revenue fell from $9.04B to $6.26B (−31%).
- HDD segment GM fell from 29.4% to 24.1% (20.7% in Q4 FY23).
- Total WDC GAAP operating loss was −$1.29B and GAAP net loss −$1.68B (flash losses magnified this).
- Headcount fell ~12k (65k → 53k).
- WDC raised ~$900M convertible preferred from Apollo/Elliott (2023) [M].
- Credit stayed high-yield.

**Now: much better positioned** [E]
1. **Net cash** of ~$0.5B, versus ~$5.6B net debt in 2024. Investment-grade rating. No long-term debt.
2. **Lean opex** of ~$1.6B/yr. Breakeven revenue at a 40% GM ≈ $4B/yr, versus a ~$17.5B run-rate. Even a 2023-style collapse (−30-40% revenue, GM to ~35%) leaves WD profitable [E].
3. **LTAs** with exabyte commitments and visibility into 2028-31 cushion volume. Pricing could still reset as LTAs renew.
4. **No unit-capacity build.** Capex is focused on density, so supply can be held tight. Fixed-cost absorption remains a risk if units fall.
5. **HDD is now ~90% nearline cloud** (vs ~60% pre-2023). It is less exposed to PC and consumer decline, but more exposed to hyperscaler capex cycles and inventory digestion.
6. **Variable shareholder returns.** Buybacks of ~$1B/qtr can be cut to preserve cash; the dividend is small (~$250M/yr).

**Survival verdict** [E]: WD would very likely survive even a severe HDD downturn intact. In a downturn the levers are margin compression, buybacks paused, and targeted R&D/SG&A cuts with layoffs, as in 2023. A going-concern threat is not plausible on the current balance sheet.

## 4. Technology analysis

### 4.1 Current product technology

| Technology | Status |
|---|---|
| **ePMR (energy-assisted PMR)** | WD's bridge technology: a current-assisted write head on conventional media. Gives an incremental areal-density gain without the HAMR media/laser change |
| **OptiNAND** | On-drive flash (iNAND) used for metadata and repeatable-runout data. Frees disk space and enabled ~20% capacity uplift with ePMR/UltraSMR (2021 → today) [R-Quartr/M] |
| **11-platter platform** | WD's 26-32TB drives use 11 disks [M/Fintool]. The roadmap goes to 12 platters (2027) and 14 platters (thinner disks enabled by the low-profile laser) [Fintool] |
| **UltraSMR** | WD's enhanced host-managed SMR with large-block ECC: about +2-4TB over CMR per drive at the same platters [M]. Adopted by the 3 largest customers; 2 meet nearly all EB needs with it [C] |
| **Capacities shipping** | 26TB CMR / 32TB UltraSMR (2024-25) [R-Quartr]. 40TB UltraSMR ePMR in volume production since Jun 2026 quarter, at 2-3 customers [C]. Yields "in the 90% range" [C] |

### 4.2 Roadmap (Feb 2026 Innovation Day + Oct 2026 call)

| Year (calendar) | ePMR / CMR-SMR | HAMR | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 32TB UltraSMR (11 platters) | 2 hyperscalers testing | — |
| 2026 | 40TB UltraSMR ePMR, volume 2H | 44TB qualification (4 customers in Apr 2026) | High-Bandwidth Drive sampling |
| 2027 | ePMR toward 60TB using HAMR-derived innovations, no added power | Gen-1 44TB: qualification completes with 2 hyperscalers in CQ1'27; volume 1H CY27; 2 more customers start qualification 1H'27. Gen-2: 5TB/platter × 11 disks (≈55TB [E]), qualification starts 2H CY27 | Power-optimized HDD (−20% power) in qualification; open-API software layer |
| 2028 | ~80TB | 10TB per platter | Dual Pivot (2 actuator sets on separate pivots, ~2x sequential IO) |
| 2029 | — | 100TB | — |
| Beyond | — | 140TB potential (14 × 10TB) | Long-term goal >100TB "2030+" (Feb 2025) |

**Key enabling technology**
- **Proprietary vertically-emitting laser:** ~6 years in development. It brings higher efficiency, a lower profile (permitting 14 platters) and pre-integration test for yield [Fintool].
- **Shared architecture:** ePMR and HAMR share a common drive architecture, easing manufacturing and customer transitions [R].

**Performance tech**

| Technology | Status |
|---|---|
| High-Bandwidth Drive | Simultaneous read/write across multiple heads and tracks: up to 2x bandwidth, path to 8x. Samples are with multiple customers [R/C] |
| Dual Pivot | 2x sequential IO, due 2028 |
| Combined | ~4x sequential IO, keeping IO-per-TB constant at 100TB |

- **Pricing:** HAMR is priced per TB, at no premium over ePMR [C].

### 4.3 Multi-actuator, partnerships and vertical integration

- **Multi-actuator:** WD's Dual Pivot differs from Seagate's MACH.2 split-actuator. It uses a second independent pivot and actuator, and claims reduced disk spacing allows more platters [R].
- **Vertical integration:** WD makes its own heads (wafer fab in Fremont/San Jose, HGA in Asia) and media. Management cites this as an edge over Toshiba, which partly relies on third-party heads and media [C/B&F].
- **HAMR partnerships:**
  - No named partners for HAMR components were disclosed. WD says it works with "internal manufacturing assets and vendors" for the ramp [C].
  - A TDK or Resonac HAMR partnership for WD could not be verified in this research. TDK/SAE supplies heads and Resonac supplies media to other HDD makers [M]. WD's laser is in-house.
  - The 10-K flags heavy reliance on sole-source suppliers for critical components [Autobound].

### 4.4 R&D spend versus Seagate

- **WD:** R&D $994M in FY2025 and $1,161M in FY2026 (GAAP) [R]. This is all HDD now.
- **Seagate:** R&D is roughly $0.7-0.8B per year (FY2025) [M, not re-verified]. On this basis, WD out-spends Seagate on HDD R&D in absolute dollars, though Seagate's HAMR investment dates back ~20 years.

### 4.5 Who leads?

**HAMR: Seagate leads** [M]
- Seagate has shipped HAMR (Mozaic 3+, 30-36TB) to hyperscalers since 2024-25 and announced ~4TB/platter-class products.
- WD's first HAMR (44TB) reaches volume about 1.5-2 years later (1H CY2027).

**ePMR, SMR and near-term capacity per drive: WD leads or is at parity** [E]
- WD has extracted far more from non-HAMR technology (32TB → 40TB ePMR, 60TB planned).
- That buys time and avoids HAMR yield risk. Its Gen-1 HAMR at 44TB would leapfrog Seagate's then-current capacity points.

**Execution risk** [E]
- WD's plan stacks several new items in 2027-28: Gen-1 and Gen-2 HAMR, 12-14 platters, and Dual Pivot.
- Any HAMR qualification slip would cede share and pricing to Seagate.
- The 10-K explicitly flags HAMR ramp margin risk [Autobound].

**Overall:** a two-horse technology race. Seagate leads on HAMR maturity; WD leads on ePMR/SMR extension, roadmap breadth (HBD, Dual Pivot) and now R&D dollars. Toshiba is the laggard (MAMR/HAMR via partners) [M/C].

---

## 5. Outlook and key risks (analyst view) [E]

**Base case**
- FY2027 revenue of roughly $19-21B, given Q1 at $4.38B, Q2 guided at $5.1B, and a continued supply-tight market.
- Non-GAAP operating margin ~50%; EPS ~$22-25.

**Bull factors**
- AI data lakes, inference logs and "physical AI" sustain >25% nearline EB growth [C].
- The HAMR ramp adds ~40-55TB per drive.
- Industry supply discipline in a duopoly-plus-Toshiba structure.

**Bear factors**
- Pricing (+28% Y/Y per TB) is at a cyclical extreme.
- Hyperscaler digestion, LTA re-pricing, Toshiba adding units, a HAMR slip, and DRAM/NAND input inflation.
- Top-3 customers are 44% of revenue.
- The stock already discounts a lot: it sold off ~7% after the beat.

**Employment angle (for later sections)**
- WD is lean (~40k people, ~88% in Asia). U.S. engineering is concentrated in San Jose and Irvine.
- Layoffs have been targeted even in boom times (Jan 2026 San Jose).
- Opex is tightly managed at ~9% of revenue. That suggests strong ability to pay and stock-comp upside in the up-cycle, but a historical pattern of cuts in downturns (2019, 2023).
