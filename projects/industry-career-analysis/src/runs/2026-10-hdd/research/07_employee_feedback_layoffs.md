# 07 — Employee Feedback, Layoffs, RTO and Offshoring at HDD Companies (2019–2026)

*Prepared 28 October 2026 for a 15-year HDD servo engineer weighing long-term employment. Reviews are paraphrased. Note on method: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through this task. Seagate, Western Digital and Toshiba are covered from sources fetched in this session. Coverage of Sandisk, Marvell, Broadcom and TDK/SAE is thin, and the items marked **[background, unverified this session]** come from prior knowledge and should be checked before they are relied on.*

**Bottom line for a senior servo engineer:**
- HDD makers cut deeply and quickly when the cycle turns down: 2016–17, 2020, and especially Oct 2022–2023.
- In-house HDD R&D in the US has shrunk for a decade. Examples: Seagate Shakopee went from more than 1,000 to about 775 staff in 2016, and Minnesota sites were consolidated in 2020. Seagate Longmont R&D was cut in 2016–17. WD Irvine, San Jose and Milpitas had WARN notices in 2023–24.
- The workforce is now overwhelmingly in Asia: about 25,000 of Seagate's ~30,000 staff and about 88% of WD's ~40,000.
- The 2025–26 AI and nearline boom has changed the mood. Stock prices are high, and there were no large layoff rounds in the sources found for that period. Historically, though, these companies cut even core R&D in the next downturn.
- Senior servo and firmware specialists are scarce, and that scarcity is real protection. It is not immunity: the 2022–23 Seagate rounds reportedly hit top performers too.

### 3.2 Recurring themes (paraphrased)

**Seagate**
- Pros:
  - Strong work-life balance with little forced overtime for salaried engineers.
  - Friendly, knowledgeable colleagues and deep disk-drive engineering expertise.
  - Good benefits, including an HSA.
  - Interesting, hard technical problems (HAMR, servo, mechanics).
- Cons:
  - Repeated layoff rounds and a sense of never knowing whether you will be employed next quarter. This is the lowest-rated category on Indeed.
  - Weak upper management.
  - Pay below the semiconductor and big-tech market. Bonuses and promotions freeze in downturns, and RSU value swings with a cyclical stock.
  - Advancement depends heavily on your manager.
  - One Blind reviewer called the company strong in drives but in "slow decline." That view predates the 2025 boom.

**Western Digital**
- Pros: suitable for a long, stable career, and engineering at the HGST-heritage sites is well regarded.
- Cons:
  - A hard ceiling on pay.
  - Culture friction after the SanDisk merger.
  - Furloughs and rolling layoffs in 2022–23.
  - Teams increasingly spread across sites, with work shifting to India and Asia.

**Toshiba**
- Pros: hybrid flexibility and autonomy, feeling respected, pay competitive in some US subsidiaries, a CEO who is well rated after the restructuring.
- Cons:
  - Limited advancement, especially in smaller subsidiaries.
  - Many meetings and a slow, consensus-driven, seniority-based Japanese culture.
  - An older workforce.
  - Early-retirement pressure on staff aged 50 and over in Japan.

**Site notes**
- **Longmont, CO** (Seagate): historically a major R&D site for servo, firmware and recording. It had about 1,530 staff in 2016, and its R&D was cut in 2016–17.
- **Shakopee, MN** (Seagate): heads and media R&D, consolidated into one Minnesota site in 2020.
- **Fremont, CA** (Seagate): head wafer and engineering staff. WARN notices in 2020 and 2022–23. Blind says engineering works on site while other functions are mostly remote.
- **San Jose, Milpitas and Irvine, CA** (WD): all had 2023–24 WARN notices.
- **Rochester, MN** (former IBM/HGST): historically a disk R&D site with long-term downsizing. No recent data found.

## 4. Return-to-office, remote work and offshoring

- **RTO:**
  - Neither Seagate nor WD has published a formal company-wide RTO mandate in trackers such as BuildRemote, where WD is listed as "not stated publicly."
  - Blind reports that Seagate Fremont engineering is largely on site because of lab and hardware work, while marketing is hybrid at a few days a week.
  - Toshiba reviewers praise hybrid flexibility.
  - For a servo engineer, expect lab-centric on-site work with some hybrid days. Fully remote roles are rare.
- **Offshoring:**
  - Seagate has about 25,000 of its ~30,000 employees in Asia.
  - WD has about 88% of ~40,000 in APAC and 11% in the Americas.
  - Seagate engineers on Blind ask about Singapore packages, which signals that senior firmware roles exist there.
  - WD enterprise SSD and firmware teams now include India.
  - Over 2016–2023 the pattern was the same: US R&D cut in each downturn while Asian design centers (Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, India, China) held steady or grew.
  - The US keeps advanced technology roles (HAMR, servo architecture, heads/media), but the junior and sustaining work goes offshore.

## 5. Sentiment shift in 2025–2026

- **Financial turnaround:**
  - Blind News (Oct 2025) shows Seagate beating forecasts on AI-driven nearline demand, with roughly $2.7B quarterly revenue guidance.
  - A Nov 2025 item noted that $100 invested in Seagate stock ten years earlier had grown to about $729.
  - The Glassdoor compensation sub-score is up 6% year over year.
- **Layoffs:** no large layoff announcements from Seagate or WD were found for 2025–2026. WD's realignment charges have fallen to only about $3M a quarter.
- **Reviews:** a 2026 WD review describes the company as a good long-term career, and a May 2026 Toshiba staff-engineer review cites strong business performance and budget.
- **What to expect:** bonuses and RSU vesting values are probably much higher in FY25–26 than in FY23, when bonuses were reportedly zero. The 2025–26 hiring data and bonus payouts could not be verified because the search budget ran out.
- **Caveat:** structurally, both companies run lean, capital-disciplined models with roughly 85–90% of staff in Asia. Management has shown it will cut US R&D quickly in a downturn: Seagate cut about 8% of staff in Oct 2022 alone, and its 2023 plans together reached up to about 12%. Expect cuts again if nearline demand corrects.

---

## 6. Implications for a 15-year HDD servo engineer

1. **Survivability:** senior servo specialists are among the least replaceable HDD R&D staff, and the talent pool is shrinking and aging. That improves the odds of surviving a layoff round but does not guarantee it.
2. **Timing:** joining during a boom such as 2025–26 means good bonuses and RSUs, but it also means more exposure if a downturn hits in 2027–28.
3. **Location:**
   - In the US, Longmont (Seagate) and San Jose (WD) are the remaining core servo sites.
   - Singapore and Thailand offer growing seniority but lower pay.
   - Toshiba Japan is risky for anyone aged 50 or over.
4. **Pay:** HDD companies pay below semiconductor and big-tech firms. Some Toshiba US reviewers say pay is better than at their previous employer. HDD SoC roles at Marvell and Broadcom pay more but have very small teams.
5. **Culture:** good work-life balance, a collegial and older engineering culture, slow promotion, and morale that rises and falls with the industry cycle.
