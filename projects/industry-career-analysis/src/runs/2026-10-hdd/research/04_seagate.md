## 1. Executive summary

- **Seagate is in its strongest financial position ever.** FQ1 FY27 revenue was **$4.23B (+61% YoY)**, non-GAAP gross margin **57.8%**, non-GAAP operating margin **50.7%**, and non-GAAP EPS **$7.68** [R]. Guidance for FQ2 FY27 is **$4.8B ±$0.1B** revenue and **$9.20 ±$0.20** EPS, with operating margin heading to the mid-50s [R].
- **FY2026 results** [R]:
  - Revenue $12.20B (+34%).
  - Non-GAAP gross margin 46.1% and non-GAAP operating margin 36.5%.
  - GAAP net income $3.18B.
  - Free cash flow $3.1B, a record.
- **Supply discipline is the core strategy.** Seagate is **not adding unit capacity**. It grows exabytes about 25% a year by raising areal density (HAMR) instead. The vast majority of nearline exabytes are **allocated under LTAs through calendar 2028**, some cloud agreements run to 2030, and price and configuration are fixed through CY2027 [R]. Revenue per TB is rising, which reverses decades of price erosion.
- **Technology: Seagate is the only HDD maker shipping HAMR at scale.**
  - Mozaic 3+ (about 30 to 36TB) is qualified at all major CSPs.
  - Mozaic 4+ (up to 44TB) is ramping at the two largest CSPs.
  - HAMR is targeted at about 50% of nearline EB exiting CY2026.
  - Mozaic 5+ (50TB+) is due for qualification in late CY2027.
  - The lab has demonstrated **8TB per disk**, and the long-term target is 10TB per disk and 100TB drives [R].
- **Balance sheet risk is largely gone.** Gross debt fell from about $5.0B (FY25) to about **$1.6B** after October 2026 repayments, net leverage is 0.2x, and the company expects to be net cash by the end of CY2026. Management now targets returning about 75% of FCF to shareholders [R].
- **Downturn lesson.** In FY2023, revenue fell 37% to $7.38B, gross margin fell to 18.3%, and the GAAP net loss was $529M. Seagate cut about 3,000 jobs (October 2022) and up to 12% of staff (April 2023) [R]. Headcount fell from 40,000 (FY22) to 30,000 (FY24) and has stayed flat at about 30,000 through FY26 despite record profits [R].
- **Stock.** STX closed at **$667.85** on October 27, 2026, about +150% year to date. It fell 5 to 8% after hours on the FQ1 print despite beating estimates. The 52-week range is about $218 to $1,145 [R]. Market cap is about **$145 to 155B** [D] (230M shares).

### 2.5 Customer concentration, LTAs and supply discipline

**Concentration**
- One customer was about **14% of FY26 revenue** [R, 10-K].
- Top hyperscalers (cloud service providers) dominate nearline demand. The 10-K says key OEM and hyperscale customers make up "a large portion" of mass-capacity revenue.

**Build-to-order and LTAs** [R]:
- The vast majority of nearline EB is allocated through **CY2028**, with some cloud agreements to **2030**.
- Configuration and pricing are locked for CY2027 shipments, and CY2028 negotiations are under way.
- Customers want visibility to 2029 and beyond.
- Preferential-price volume to one customer went to zero in the September 2026 quarter.

**Capacity strategy** [R]:
- No new unit capacity. Capex stays at 4 to 6% of revenue (FY26 capex $569M, 4.7% of revenue).
- Mosley: "increasing the amount of data stored on every disk we produce is the fastest, most capital-efficient path."
- Target: **mid-20s% nearline EB supply CAGR**.
- Management is "building to demand to avoid overbuilding" and is not seeing hyperscaler power or shell-construction delays.
- When an analyst noted that peers plan higher capex, management said its 4 to 6% is sufficient.

## 3. Downturn resilience

### 3.1 What happened in FY2023, the last downturn

Cloud customers digested inventory, COVID-era PC and edge demand collapsed, and industry pricing eroded. As a result [R]:
- **Revenue fell 37%**, from $11.66B to $7.38B. FY24 fell a further 11% to $6.55B, a **44% peak-to-trough decline**.
- Gross margin fell to **18.3%** (FY23) and the operating loss was **-$342M**. That included the $300M BIS penalty; excluding it, operating income was roughly break-even [D].
- Net loss was **-$529M**.
- The company took factory **underutilization charges** in FY23 and FY24, a cost of its vertically integrated model with high fixed costs in heads and media fabs.
- It responded with two restructurings, a 25% headcount reduction (40K to 30K), executive salaries cut to zero for six months, and R&D cut from $941M to $654M. The dividend was maintained, which kept leverage elevated (gross debt about $5B).

### 3.2 Resilience now [E]

**Much stronger than in 2022:**
- Moving to net cash, with debt of about $1.6B against $5B+ in FCF capacity.
- About 50% operating margins give a large cushion.
- LTAs fix volume allocations through CY2028 and price through CY2027.
- Data Center is about 81% of revenue, while the most volatile consumer/PC exposure has shrunk.

**Stress test** [E]: suppose revenue falls 35% from the FY27 run rate (about $18B annualized to about $11.7B) and gross margin compresses to about 35% (still above the FY25 level). Operating income would still be about $2.9B, because opex is only about $1.2 to 1.3B a year. Seagate would stay solidly profitable.

**Remaining risks:**
- High fixed-cost fabs, so underutilization charges would return.
- About 14% single-customer concentration.
- Hyperscalers' bargaining power if AI capex slows. LTAs typically allow for volume renegotiation.
- Exposure to the HDD pricing cycle after a historic price upswing.
- HAMR yield and scrap issues.
- SSD (QLC) substitution at the high end if NAND prices fall.
- Tariffs and geopolitics (China assembly, Middle East).
- Litigation: a $175M securities class action (Seagate's net share about $105M, final hearing November 2026) and the Lambeth patent retrial [R].

## 4. Technology analysis

### 4.1 HAMR and the Mozaic platform

| Platform | Per-disk capacity | Drive capacity (10 platters) | Status as of October 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mozaic 3+ | ~3 to 3.6TB | 30 to 36TB (Exos M) | Qualified and in volume at all major cloud customers. Five major CSPs had qualified by Q1 FY26; more than 1M units shipped that quarter [R] |
| **Mozaic 4+** | ~4.4TB | **up to 44TB** | Revenue shipments began in late March 2026. Qualified and in production with the **two largest global CSPs**; more CSP and enterprise OEM qualifications expected "over the next few months." Expected to be about 50% of HAMR EB exiting CY2026 [R] |
| **Mozaic 5+** | 5TB+ | **50TB+** | Customer qualification shipments in **late CY2027**. Management says the transition "could come faster than planned" and will discuss it in FY2028 [R] |
| Lab and roadmap | **8TB per disk demonstrated in the lab**; target 10TB per disk | ~100TB | Long-term roadmap [R] |

**HAMR penetration** [R]:
- About 40% of nearline EB run rate at June 2026.
- About 50% expected exiting CY2026.
- A previously stated goal of about 70% by June 2027, which management says is "on track for future goals."

**Economics** [R]: the CFO says moving from 3TB to 4TB per disk is margin-accretive. Unit volumes stay flat while each drive carries more terabytes, so cost per TB falls and revenue per unit rises.

**Mozaic 4+ ingredients** [R]:
- In-house designed and manufactured **HAMR laser / nanophotonics**, using **vertically integrated photonics**.
- Next-generation suspension architecture.
- An enhanced in-house **SoC**.
- Seagate's claimed efficiency for a 1EB deployment versus 30TB conventional drives: about 47% better infrastructure efficiency and about 0.8M kWh a year less energy [R].

**Areal density** [D]: about 4.4TB per 3.5-inch platter is about 2 to 2.2 Tb/in². The 10TB-per-disk goal implies about 5 Tb/in² or more.

### 4.2 Vertical integration

Seagate designs and fabricates its own:
- **Recording heads**: wafer fabs in Northern Ireland and Minnesota.
- **Media**: Singapore.
- **HAMR lasers and near-field transducers (NFT)**: in-house nanophotonics.
- **SoCs and firmware**.
- **Final assembly**.

In March 2025 it acquired **Intevac** for about $88M. Intevac makes thin-film deposition equipment for media, which secures HAMR media tooling [R].

Vertical integration gives a cost and IP edge during the HAMR transition. It also creates the fixed-cost and underutilization exposure seen in FY23.

### 4.3 MACH.2 dual actuator [BK]

The Exos 2X series (2X14, 2X18) uses two independent actuators to roughly double IOPS per drive. It addresses the falling IOPS per TB of very large drives and has shipped to select hyperscalers since about 2021 to 2022. It is a niche product today but becomes more relevant at 40 to 50TB and above. Seagate has indicated it can be combined with HAMR in future products [E].

### 4.4 R&D intensity vs competitors

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| R&D, FY2019 | $991M (~9.5% of revenue) |
| R&D, FY2026 | $755M (~6.2%) |
| R&D, FQ1 FY27 | $200M (~4.7% of revenue) |

All values are [R/D].

**Competitor comparison** [BK]: Western Digital's HDD-only R&D (after the 2025 SanDisk flash spin-off) is in a similar range of roughly $0.7 to 0.8B, and Toshiba is smaller. Absolute R&D dollars are similar between Seagate and Western Digital. Seagate's lead comes from **about 20 years of cumulative HAMR investment**: HAMR research since the early 2000s and first HAMR drive demonstrations around 2017 to 2019.

### 4.5 Patents and IP [BK/E]

- Seagate holds one of the largest HDD patent portfolios (thousands of US patents) with a large HAMR, NFT and laser-integration cluster.
- The Lambeth patent-infringement case (concerning head technology) was remanded for retrial [R].
- No evidence was found of IP barriers blocking competitors' HAMR. Western Digital and Toshiba pursue HAMR through their own and partner (TDK, Showa Denko/Resonac) supply chains.

### 4.6 Other products

**SSDs** [BK]:
- Nytro enterprise SSDs and FireCuda/IronWolf consumer SSDs. These use third-party NAND and are often based on Phison controllers.
- Small and not strategic. Flash is not a reporting segment.

**Systems and services:** Exos CORVAULT and other storage enclosures, and Lyve (mass-storage data transport and cloud). These are small contributors inside Edge/IoT and Data Center [BK].

**Strategic stance:** Seagate is a pure-play HDD and mass-capacity storage company. It has no NAND fab exposure.

### 4.7 Who leads in technology? [E]

**Seagate leads on areal density and HAMR commercialization.** It is the only vendor shipping HAMR in volume, with millions of units, two generations qualified, and a third due in 2027.

**Western Digital** has pursued ePMR/OptiNAND with SMR, plus UltraSMR, to reach about 26 to 32TB CMR/SMR. It has publicly targeted HAMR around 2026 to 2027 [BK, not verified this session]. On the FQ1 call, an analyst noted that peers are planning higher capex, which may indicate Western Digital is adding capacity or HAMR tooling.

**Toshiba** pursues MAMR and HAMR, but its ramp is behind.

**Net assessment:** Seagate holds a roughly 1 to 2 generation (about 12 to 24 month) density lead at the drive level. The lead has narrowed slightly in market impact because Western Digital's conventional drives remained competitive on cost per TB and qualification simplicity.

**Key risk:** HAMR scrap and yield during transitions, which already appears as slower cost-per-TB declines.

## 5. Outlook and analyst view [E]

**Base case**
- FY2027 revenue of about **$18 to 19B**: Q1 $4.23B, Q2 guidance $4.8B, and continued moderate growth in the second half. Management expects FY27 growth to exceed FY26's 34% [R].
- Non-GAAP operating margin of about 50 to 55%.
- FCF of about $7 to 8B.

**Medium term (FY2028 to FY2030)**
- Exabytes grow at about 20 to 25% CAGR.
- Pricing per TB normalizes from flat to slightly down once Western Digital HAMR and additional supply arrive. The LTA pricing reset for CY2028 is the key watch item.
- Margins likely settle at about 40 to 50% gross, still far above the historic 25 to 30%.

**Bull case:** AI and data-retention demand keeps the industry supply-constrained. Seagate's density lead sustains a pricing premium and the company becomes a structurally high-margin "toll road" on cloud storage.

**Bear case:**
- A hyperscaler capex pause or digestion cycle, as in 2022 to 2023.
- QLC SSD encroachment if NAND oversupplies.
- Western Digital closes the HAMR gap.
- Pricing reverts. Even so, with net cash and an opex base of about $1.2B a year, Seagate would very likely remain profitable [E].

**Employment angle** [E]: the company is generating record profit per employee while holding headcount flat at about 30,000. Its history shows aggressive cuts in downturns: about 25% of staff in FY23 to FY24 and R&D sites consolidated (Shakopee). Engineering roles tied to HAMR (heads, media, photonics, servo and firmware for high-TPI tracks) are the most strategic. Legacy Edge/IoT and US R&D sites have been the most exposed in past cuts.
