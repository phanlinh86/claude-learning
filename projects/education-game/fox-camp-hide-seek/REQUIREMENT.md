# Pip's Camp Hide & Seek · Cáo Pip Chơi Trốn Tìm (education game, 2026-11-03)

Made unattended with `/build-education-game random 4` (seed 2026-11-03, America/Los_Angeles). The roller skipped the
families of the last 3 games (pop, memory, trace+path) and rationed *container*. Rolled: mechanic **hide-and-seek:
friends hide behind bushes/doors; tap to peek and find the one asked for** (family **seek**), theme **mountain camping**,
helper **a baby fox**, weather **spring → summer → autumn → winter**, skills for age 4 **colours · shapes · counting 1–5 ·
animals**. No swap: a fox cub playing hide-and-seek with forest friends around a mountain campsite through the four
seasons is one coherent story (the hiding places change with the season).

## Pitch
Pip, a baby fox in a teal camping bandana, plays hide-and-seek with the animal friends at a mountain campsite. Pip covers
both eyes and counts "One… two… three!" while the friends scamper and duck behind bushes, tents, leaf piles and snowmen.
A little clue always peeks out: a red woolly hat, a star flag, a sign with three stars, a pair of cow ears. Pip asks
"Where is the friend in the RED hat?" and the child taps a hiding place: it rustles open and — boo! — out pops a friend.
The right friend jumps out with a cheer and hops over to sit by the campfire; a wrong friend giggles "Not me! I'm in
blue!" and ducks back down. Find everybody, the campfire flares with sparkles, and the next game of hide-and-seek begins.
The campsite changes from spring blossoms to summer tents, autumn leaves and winter snow.

## How it differs from the last games
No dragging into containers, no popping, no card grid, no flying. The gesture is **tap a hiding place to peek**: things
are hidden and the child reads a partial clue (only a hat, a flag tip or a pair of ears shows). The layout is a
**campsite meadow seen from the side with hiding places in two depth rows**; the reward is **a reveal** (the hiding
place bursts open, the friend pops up, jumps out and hops to the campfire) and the round ends with a **campfire dance**.
Every round opens with a short watching moment: the friends run and hide while Pip counts aloud (counting practice).

## Audience and learning goals
- Default age 4 (3–5 range); the parent picks 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7+ in settings.
- Topics by age (4 chapters per session, one per season, easiest first; chapter topics rotate between sessions):
  - 3: colours of hats (2 places) · shapes on flags (2) · big & small friends · animals by their ears (2)
  - 4: colours (3) · shapes (3) · counting 1–5 (signs with stars; "count up" rounds find 1, then 2, then 3) · animals (3, "Who says moo?" from round 3)
  - 5: shapes in mixed colours (4) · counting 1–10 · capital letters · animals (4)
  - 6: capital ↔ small letters · first letter of a word · adding within 10 · counting to 12
  - 7+: adding within 20 · taking away within 10 · first letter · odd & even
- A round = one game of hide-and-seek: N hiding places, N friends, N−1 questions (warm-up round: 2 places, 1 question).
- No losing, no timers. A wrong friend giggles and says kindly what it has ("I have the blue hat! Find red!").
  After 2 misses on one question the right hiding place glows. Stars only go up: 3 per round without a miss, 2 with one,
  1 otherwise; +1 for Talk time.

## Core loop
Friends hop in and wave → Pip covers eyes and counts 1-2-3 (friends scurry behind hiding places, clues peek out) →
Pip asks → the child taps a hiding place → it rustles open and the friend pops up → right: the friend jumps out, cheers
and hops to the campfire bench (Pip names it and counts the finds) → next question … → all found: campfire dance,
stars → next round. 4 rounds per chapter, then **Talk time** (one picture question, optional microphone), then the
season changes. After 4 chapters: a winter-night campfire party with fireworks and "Play again".
One surprise round per chapter: two hidden friends swap places in front of the child ("Whoa, they switched!"), or a
squirrel pops out of a tree (tap it for a sparkle shower).

## Voice
Every instruction spoken (English en-US or Vietnamese vi-VN) and shown in a speech bubble; tap Pip or the bubble to
hear it again. Pip counts aloud while the friends hide, names what popped out, praises with the child's name, never
negative words.
